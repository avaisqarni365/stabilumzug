General guidance, not legal or official advice. Rules differ from city to city and can change. Check with the responsible office.

How bulky waste usually works in Germany

Bulky waste (Sperrmüll) is regulated locally. In most cities you book a collection date with the municipal waste service, often several weeks in advance. On the agreed day you place the items at the kerb. Each city decides what counts as bulky waste, how much is free and what it costs. Check your city's website or waste service before you start sorting.

Some things are not bulky waste and go to the recycling centre (Wertstoffhof) instead: electrical appliances, hazardous waste such as paint or chemicals, and construction waste.

The sequence that works

  1. While making your inventory list, note what will not move.
  2. Sort it: bulky waste, electrical appliances, things to sell or give away, ordinary household waste.
  3. Book the city's collection early enough that it falls before moving day.
  4. Take electrical appliances and hazardous waste to the recycling centre.
  5. Start selling and giving away early, because buyers need time to collect.

Why a late appointment can be costly

The old flat must be handed back empty. Anything still there at the handover can be charged to the tenant. A collection booked too late is therefore one of the most common mistakes. Plan the date as soon as you know your moving date, and add it to your moving checklist.
