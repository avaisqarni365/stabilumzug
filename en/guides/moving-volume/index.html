More people, more things, but not twice as much

With every person, furniture, clothes and kitchen goods grow. But not evenly: a second adult adds less than the first, and children add toys and bulky items. The table shows typical ranges.

HouseholdApprox. volume (m³)Approx. boxesTypical vehicleTypical helpers

Approximate planning values, not measurements. Your furniture and household can differ considerably. Furniture density, cellar contents and dismantled furniture change the real volume. Rely on a written quote.

Why weight matters too

Every vehicle has a payload limit. Packed household goods weigh roughly a few hundred kilograms per cubic metre. A four-person household can therefore reach several tonnes in total. Heavy appliances and book boxes are also what most often causes injuries on stairs.

From estimate to plan

  • Start with the row for your household size.
  • Refine it with your inventory list, especially for large wardrobes and a full cellar.
  • Check floor and access at both addresses. They often decide the number of helpers more than the volume does.
